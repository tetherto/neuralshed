# T11 channel correspondence

Participant `braingate2/T11` appears in two Human Motor datasets: [karpowicz_2025](../../src/neuralshed/datasets/karpowicz_2025/README.md) and [gusman_2025](../../src/neuralshed/datasets/gusman_2025/README.md). T11 has two 96-electrode Utah arrays in the left precentral gyrus. Both Studies emit 192 columns per neural feature in source order, one block of 96 per array:

| columns | karpowicz_2025 | gusman_2025 |
|---|---|---|
| 0-95 | `lateral_array` (inferred) | `lateral_array` (`Lateral` in the file) |
| 96-191 | `medial_array` (inferred) | `medial_array` (`Medial` in the file) |

This page tests whether column `i` is the same electrode in both. No recording is in both releases: karpowicz_2025 holds only radial-8 calibration and Simon blocks, and gusman_2025 only Gesture Hero and drag-and-drop blocks. No day is in both either, if block numbers are unique within a day: every gusman_2025 session reuses block numbers of both karpowicz_2025 days (for example block 3, 7, 8, 9 or 12). gusman_2025 publishes no dates, so the gaps between its sessions and karpowicz_2025 are unknown.

| dataset | sessions | post-implant day | tasks | neural features |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| karpowicz_2025 | 2 | 153, 427 | radial-8 calibration, Simon sequence game | crossings, 20 ms, counts |
| gusman_2025 | 4 | not published | Gesture Hero (2), drag-and-drop (2) | non-causal crossings at -3.5 x RMS, spike-band power and five LFP bands, 20 ms, z-scored over a rolling 3 min window |

## Conclusions

- gusman_2025 states that columns 0-95 are the lateral array and 96-191 the medial array. No source states an electrode order, and karpowicz_2025 states no array order.
- The two releases share one column order at the array level. In every comparison between them the stored order beats the array swap and all other orders of the six 32-channel banks.
- karpowicz_2025 columns 0-95 fall on the array gusman_2025 names `Lateral`. This supports the Study's lateral/medial assignment as a candidate.
- Electrode order within each array is a candidate. Columns 96-191 match well above chance in every pairing. Columns 0-95 do so between karpowicz_2025 and the drag-and-drop sessions, but not in the two Gesture Hero sessions. There, columns 0-95 disagree with every other session in crossing rates and in threshold-free measures, at rest as well as during the task. The evidence points to a change in how that array was recorded in those sessions, not to a reordered array. Their electrode order is not established.
- Across sessions within each release the array order holds, and the electrode order cannot be confirmed.

## Method

Each session is summarized per feature by the channel correlation matrix at 100 ms, pooled over blocks after removing each block's mean and clipping each column at its 0.1st and 99.9th percentiles, and by each column's mean crossing rate. Two sessions are compared:

- Array order: Pearson correlation of the upper triangles under all 720 orders of the six 32-channel banks. The stored order and the array swap are among them.
- Centred score: the matrix score after subtracting each array-pair submatrix's mean, against 500 random orders within each array.
- Per-array score: the same within one array's 96 x 96 submatrix, against 500 random orders of that array.
- Row assignment: per array, each column's correlation profile in one session is matched to every column's profile in the other with `linear_sum_assignment`. Chance is about 1%.
- Graph matching: `scipy.optimize.quadratic_assignment` (FAQ, barycenter and four random starts).
- Rate score: Pearson correlation of `log(1 + mean)` per column, for all 192 columns and per array, against 1,000 random orders within each array.

karpowicz_2025 releases crossings only, so all comparisons between the releases use crossings. gusman_2025 releases each feature z-scored over a rolling window, which removes the mean. Crossing counts are integers, so within any 5 s window the released values sit on an evenly spaced grid whose lowest level is a zero count. The level and spacing give the rolling mean, and averaging over windows gives each column's mean rate. Windows holding two grids, where the rolling statistics jump, are skipped. The rate from the share of zero-count bins agrees (r = 0.95-0.997 over columns), and the first and second half of a session agree at r = 0.990 and 0.992.

Calibration on the two halves of each session (alternate blocks):

- The stored order ranks first of 720 for every session and feature: crossings 0.929 and 0.934 in karpowicz_2025 and 0.425-0.978 in gusman_2025, spike-band power 0.555-0.992. The lowest values are gusman_2025 drag-and-drop session 1.
- The centred score is 0.386-0.985 against at most 0.163 for random orders within arrays.
- Row assignment returns 95-100% of columns 96-191 to themselves in every session. For crossings on columns 0-95 it returns 100% in the Gesture Hero sessions and 21-40% in the other four, where that array carries little correlated activity.
- The karpowicz_2025 rate score is 0.995 and 0.996, against -0.333 and -0.572 swapped.
- Graph matching recovers 32-61% of columns, and 0% in drag-and-drop session 1.

### Within gusman_2025

The four sessions have spike-band power and LFP bands, which karpowicz_2025 lacks. LFP band power is spatially smooth and gives each array a stable correlation pattern. Per-array matrix score (shuffle max) and row assignment, for the LFP bands labelled 0-8 and 12-16 Hz. Here row assignment compares two columns' profiles over all other columns, leaving out both columns' own entries:

| pair | columns 0-95 | columns 96-191 |
|---|---|---|
| halves of each drag-and-drop session | 0.903-0.978, 89-98% | 0.952-0.990, 100% |
| halves of each Gesture Hero session | 0.964-0.993, 26-50% | 0.619-0.997, 95-100% |
| drag-and-drop 1 / 2 | 0.844-0.936 (at most 0.183), 83-90% | 0.850-0.867 (at most 0.266), 80-93% |

Columns 96-191 keep a stable LFP pattern in all four sessions. Columns 0-95 do in the drag-and-drop sessions, but not within or between the Gesture Hero sessions, and they match the drag-and-drop sessions above the shuffles in 4 of 8 band comparisons.

Crossings: the stored order ranks first of 720 in all six pairs of sessions (0.324-0.741), and the centred score exceeds every within-array order in all six (0.157-0.633 against at most 0.131). Spike-band power ranks the stored order first in four of six pairs. Gesture Hero session 1 against both drag-and-drop sessions ranks it 2nd and 4th, behind exchanges of banks within an array, with centred scores below the shuffle maximum.

### Columns 0-95 in the Gesture Hero sessions

The crossing-rate profile of 0-95 in both Gesture Hero sessions is negatively correlated with both karpowicz_2025 days and both drag-and-drop sessions (-0.389 to -0.134 per session), and in all 20 blocks (-0.471 to -0.245 against day 427). The two Gesture Hero sessions agree with each other (0.540). Four explanations were tested.

- The task. In the no-action periods between Gesture Hero trials (70% of the bins), the profile still correlates -0.447 to -0.169 with the drag-and-drop intertrial periods and both karpowicz_2025 days. Rest and task rates within each Gesture Hero session agree at 0.987-0.991. Columns 96-191 at rest agree with the drag-and-drop intertrial periods at 0.427-0.514.
- The rate recovery or the threshold. On 0-95 of the Gesture Hero sessions a median of 98-99% of windows pass the grid check (at least 53% per column), and the Fano factor read from the grid is 1.01-1.03, as in the drag-and-drop sessions (1.01). Measures that do not depend on the crossing threshold disagree as well: the per-column correlation of crossings with spike-band power gives -0.204 to -0.004 between Gesture Hero and drag-and-drop sessions on 0-95 (0.227-0.475 on 96-191), and each column's LFP correlation with the rest of its array gives -0.049 to 0.091 (drag-and-drop 1 against 2: 0.901 and 0.917).
- A reordered array. None of the 48 bank-level orders of 0-95 (the six orders of its three banks, each bank forward or reversed) lifts the rate score above 0.075, or the LFP or crossing structure above the stored order by more than 0.010. Graph matching of the LFP structure finds a different map for each Gesture Hero and drag-and-drop pairing (0-5% of columns agree between maps), and the maps do not fix the rates consistently (-0.403 to 0.244). A real reorder would give one map that fixes both. Under the stored order, row assignment returns 3-21% of 0-95 between the Gesture Hero sessions and the other sessions in crossings, power and LFP, against about 1% by chance.
- A recording difference on that array. In the Gesture Hero sessions columns 0-95 share more activity: the median correlation of a column's crossings with the rest of the array is 0.229 and 0.257, against 0.093 and 0.137 in the drag-and-drop sessions, and 5.6-5.7% of bins have more than 32 of the 96 columns crossing together, against 0.04-0.07%. Their LFP pattern is unstable even between halves of one session (above). Leaving out those bins, or the bins the source flags as NS5 outliers, does not change the rate disagreement. Against day 427, on the same per-bin counts, the two sessions score -0.441 and -0.298 with all bins, -0.452 and -0.331 without the common bins, and -0.443 and -0.280 without the outlier bins. Columns 96-191 keep a stable LFP pattern and match the other sessions.

The data support the last explanation. Something changed how 0-95 was recorded in the Gesture Hero sessions, affecting spiking and LFP features alike, with no sign of a reorder. The paper and the authors' code state no session difference. Candidates are the common-average reference, which is rebuilt each session from the 80 lowest-variance channels of each array, and the wireless transmission of each array. The release does not allow either to be checked.

### Within karpowicz_2025

Day 153 against day 427 (274 days apart): the stored order ranks first of 720 (0.580, swap -0.112), and the centred score is 0.283 against at most 0.169. Per array, the matrix score is 0.649 (shuffle max 0.054) on 0-95 and 0.265 (0.193) on 96-191, and the rate score 0.523 (0.314) and 0.477 (0.300). Row assignment returns 22-23% of each array and graph matching 6%.

### Across sessions

Within each release, consecutive sessions keep the array order, and same-numbered columns resemble each other well beyond chance. These tests share columns and references and cannot show that every column is the same electrode from one session to the next. Whether it is depends on the recording rig, and pooling sessions within either release assumes it.

## Notes for use

- Pool T11 by column index across karpowicz_2025 and gusman_2025 at the array level. No reorder is indicated.
- karpowicz_2025's `lateral_array` / `medial_array` names agree with gusman_2025's labels as a candidate.
- Do not assume electrode correspondence for columns 0-95 of the gusman_2025 Gesture Hero sessions. Their signals on that array differ from every other T11 session, at rest and in LFP as well as in crossings.
- gusman_2025 has no dates, so a split held out by day cannot be checked across the two releases. The block numbers suggest no shared day.
- Features differ: karpowicz_2025 releases raw counts with an unstated threshold. gusman_2025 releases non-causal crossings at -3.5 x RMS after a per-array common-average reference, z-scored over a rolling 3 min window, so its mean levels are lost unless recovered as above.
- vargas_2025 (T11, not merged) is not covered.
