# T17 channel correspondence

Participant `braingate2/T17` appears in three Human Communication datasets: [jude_2026_speech](../../src/neuralshed/datasets/jude_2026_speech/README.md), [jude_2026_typing](../../src/neuralshed/datasets/jude_2026_typing/README.md) and [kunz_2025](../../src/neuralshed/datasets/kunz_2025/README.md). T17 has six 64-electrode Utah arrays in the left precentral gyrus, two each in areas 6v, 55b and 6d. Every Study emits one block of 64 columns per array, in the same order:

| columns | array | jude_2026_speech | jude_2026_typing | kunz_2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0-63 | `ventral_6v` | all days | all days | yes |
| 64-127 | `dorsal_6v` | all days | all days | yes |
| 128-191 | `ventral_55b` | all days | all days | yes |
| 192-255 | `dorsal_55b` | all days | all days | yes |
| 256-319 | `ventral_6d` | 384-column days | all days | not in the release |
| 320-383 | `dorsal_6d` | 384-column days | all days | not in the release |

This page tests whether column `i` is the same electrode in all three. No day is in two datasets.

| dataset | days | dates (post-implant day) | columns | neural features |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| jude_2026_speech | 14 | 2024-03-25 to 2024-08-13 (25-166) | 384, or 256 on 04.15-04.17 | crossings at -3.5 x SD and spike-band power, 10 ms |
| jude_2026_typing | 9 | 2024-04-24 to 2024-07-22 (55-144) | 384 | the same |
| kunz_2025 | 1 | 2024-12-09 (284) | 256 | the same |

The column order of the jude_2026_speech files changed between 2024-04-02 and 2024-04-15. Its Study permutes the three early sessions (03.25, 03.26, 04.02) into the later order. jude_2026_typing and kunz_2025 emit their files' order.

## Conclusions

- The three Studies emit one column order at the array level. The current layouts need no change.
- The area of each column range is stated by the authors' code and the in-file labels. The ventral or dorsal array within each area pair is not stated consistently and rests on the data.
- In the three early jude_2026_speech sessions, the data support the Study's permutation. The titles in the authors' 03.25 notebooks exchange ventral and dorsal within 6v and within 55b, and do not match the later sessions under that reading.
- kunz_2025's split of its single `55b` group into `ventral_55b` (128-191) and `dorsal_55b` (192-255) matches the jude datasets' order.
- Electrode order within each array is a candidate. Same-named arrays match electrode by electrode well above chance, but not for every electrode.

No source gives an electrode-level map. The kunz code places both T17 6v arrays in superior 6v and names them by their relative position, which agrees with the jude ventral-first order.

The online decoder's normalization vectors (`norm_channel_means`) match the per-column means of these file columns: 192-255 on 03.26, 128-255 on 04.02, 0-127 on every later speech day (the 6v arrays in each era), 256-383 on typing 06.03, and 0-127 plus 256-383 from typing 06.04 on (6v and 6d, as the typing paper states).

## Method

Each session is summarized per feature (crossings, spike-band power) by the column correlation matrix at 100 ms and by each column's mean. Blocks are mean-centred before pooling, and jude_2026_typing blocks are split at recording pauses. Each column is clipped at its 0.1st and 99.9th percentiles. Two sessions are compared:

- Array order: Pearson correlation of the upper triangles under every map of one session's arrays onto the other's (720 for six arrays, 360 for four onto six).
- Pair order: for each area pair, the similarity of same-named arrays against crossed ones. Similarity is the correlation of the within-array correlation pattern or of `log(1 + mean)` per column. Neither depends on an array's overall correlation level.
- Electrode order: the array-order score after subtracting each array-pair submatrix's mean, against 500 random orders within arrays, and the share of columns that row assignment returns to themselves within each array.

Calibration: the two halves of each of the 24 sessions rank identity first of all array orders for both features (margin at least 0.007).

## Results

### Array and pair order

Every pair of sessions, both features. Pair order counts comparisons where same-named arrays beat crossed ones, over two features and two measures.

| sessions | pairs | gap (days) | identity first, crossings / power | 6v | 55b | 6d |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| speech late / speech late | 28 | 1-99 | 26 / 26 | 112/112 | 112/112 | 112/112 |
| typing / typing | 36 | 1-89 | 36 / 34 | 142/144 | 144/144 | 144/144 |
| speech early / speech late | 24 | 34-141 | 21 / 20 | 92/96 | 96/96 | 96/96 |
| speech early / typing | 27 | 22-119 | 15 / 18 | 91/108 | 108/108 | 108/108 |
| speech late / typing | 72 | 6-111 | 68 / 66 | 285/288 | 288/288 | 287/288 |
| speech 256-column / speech and typing | 60 | 7-120 | 37 / 35 | 237/240 | 238/240 | - |
| kunz / speech and typing | 23 | 118-259 | 8 / 10 | 92/92 | 90/92 | - |

- Of the 120 comparisons where identity is not the best array order, 74 place a 256-column session's arrays on the 6d arrays of a 384-column session, which the area labels exclude. 18 exchange the two 6v arrays and 16 a 6v and a 6d array. The median loss is 0.03.
- kunz_2025 ranks identity first against speech 04.15 and 04.16, typing 07.09 and 07.10 and both August speech days in both features, and against typing 07.22 in crossings. Exchanging its 6v pair or its 55b pair never beats identity in any of the 46 comparisons.
- The 6v pair is the weakest: the pattern of the early 6v arrays matches the typing days at a median of 0.09 against 0.11 crossed in power. The rate profile still favours the Study order in all 54 of those comparisons.

### Early jude_2026_speech sessions

Against the eight later 384-column speech days, the same-named arrays under the Study's permutation correlate at a median of 0.16-0.54 across pairs, features and measures, and the crossed arrays at -0.20 to 0.13. Between two later days the same-named median is 0.31-0.66. The crossed pairing of 6v and 55b is the reading of the 03.25 notebook titles, and its whole-matrix score falls below identity in 121 of 126 comparisons of the early days with other sessions.

### jude_2026_typing `t17.2024.07.22`

Free-typing blocks 4 and 5 hold 11 and 5 recording pauses. Pooled as stored, their spike-band power scores 0.09 and 0.06 against 07.10 (identity 16th and 62nd of 720). Centred per continuous span, they score 0.70 and 0.28 and rank identity first, like blocks 1-3 (0.73-0.74). The pauses shift the power level, and nothing is reordered. The summaries here split at pauses.

### Electrode order

Across datasets (43 comparisons per feature, each session against the nearest session of the other datasets):

- The array-centred score exceeds every within-array order for spike-band power in 43 of 43 (0.15-0.65 against at most 0.11) and for crossings in 40 of 43. The three misses are kunz_2025 against the early speech days, 251-259 days apart.
- Row assignment returns a median of 30-51% of each array's columns to themselves for power and 6-50% for crossings, against 1.6% by chance. For crossings the 55b arrays, which record little spiking, are lowest.

## Notes for use

- Pool T17 by column index across the three datasets. kunz_2025 and the 256-column speech days hold only columns 0-255.
- No day is in two datasets, so a day-held-out split needs no cross-dataset grouping.
- All three datasets apply linear-regression referencing, threshold at -3.5 x SD and store unnormalized 10 ms features. Spike-band power halves on five arrays in the August speech sessions.
- The jude_2026_speech paper's Figure 1D statement about the ventral 6v array comes from the 03.25 notebook. Under the Study's permutation, that bar is the array the Study calls `dorsal_6v`.
- Free-typing blocks with recording pauses need per-span centring before any analysis that pools time.
- The kunz_2025 README and metadata describe the 55b split as assumed. This page supports it as a candidate.
