# T15 channel correspondence

Participant `braingate2/T15` appears in three Human Communication datasets: [card_2024](../../src/neuralshed/datasets/card_2024/README.md), [wairagkar_2025](../../src/neuralshed/datasets/wairagkar_2025/README.md) and [kunz_2025](../../src/neuralshed/datasets/kunz_2025/README.md). All three emit 256 columns per neural feature in the same order, one block of 64 per array: `ventral_6v` 0-63, `4` 64-127, `55b` 128-191, `dorsal_6v` 192-255. This page records the evidence that column `i` is the same electrode in all three.

The Human Motor dataset [singer_2025](../../src/neuralshed/datasets/singer_2025/README.md) records T15 with the same layout. It shares no recordings with the other three, so it is covered separately in [singer_2025](#singer_2025).

## Conclusions

- The current layouts are correct and need no change. All three releases use the same column order for threshold crossings and for spike-band power.
- Where two releases hold the same trials, column `i` is the same electrode in both: card_2024 and wairagkar_2025 on 25 days, and card_2024 and kunz_2025 on 2024-03-03.
- The array order `ventral_6v`, `4`, `55b`, `dorsal_6v` is stated by all three sources and agrees with the data in every comparison.
- wairagkar_2025 and kunz_2025 never recorded the same day, and no day is shared by all three. Both match card_2024 channel for channel, so they correspond through it, but only as a candidate, because the link spans different days.
- Whether a column stays the same electrode from one session to the next is a property of the recording rig. It applies to pooling days within one dataset as well, and the checks here cannot establish it at the electrode level.
- Matching correlation structure between recordings of different tasks does not recover individual electrodes. Electrode identity across releases needs the same trials in both, or a source that states the map.
- The shared trials are the same recordings released twice. [t15_repeated_trials.md](t15_repeated_trials.md) lists them and explains how to keep them out of a test set.

## Result

| pair | array level | electrode level |
|---|---|---|
| card_2024 and wairagkar_2025 | verified (`source_documented`, `same_recording_validated`) | verified on the 25 shared days, 2023-08-11 to 2023-12-29 (`same_recording_validated`) |
| card_2024 and kunz_2025 | verified (`source_documented`, `same_recording_validated`) | verified on 2024-03-03 (`same_recording_validated`). Candidate on the other kunz days |
| wairagkar_2025 and kunz_2025 | verified (`source_documented`) | candidate (`inferred`): both match card_2024 channel for channel, but on different days |
| singer_2025 and each of the other three | verified (`source_documented`, `inferred`) | candidate (`inferred`): no shared recordings |

On the shared days, a column index identifies the same electrode in both datasets of each pair, for both threshold crossings and spike-band power. Outside those days, the correspondence depends on the recording keeping one channel order from session to session (see [Across sessions](#across-sessions)).

No inspected source states an electrode-level map, and neither the card_2024 HDF5 files nor the wairagkar_2025 `.mat` files carry channel metadata. The brain-to-voice repository (`Neuroprosthetics-Lab/brain-to-voice-2025`, commit `c6f52a0`) does not mention channel order.

`analysis/Fig1D_PSTHs.m` in the kunz repository has the comment "T15 ch 23 (55b)" while column 23 falls in the `ventral_6v` block. The same comment gives "T12 ch 11" while the code plots T12 column 53, so the comment is out of date and is not evidence.

## Same-recording checks

Trials were paired by cue text and by the timing of the summed activity of all channels. Both are independent of channel order, so the pairing does not assume the answer. Each feature was then compared separately: every column of one release was correlated with every column of the other over all paired clips. Two readings of that table are reported. The best match asks whether each column correlates most with its own index. The assignment (`scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment`) asks whether the best one-to-one map of all 256 columns is the identity.

### card_2024 and kunz_2025, 2024-03-03

kunz file `sentenceData/t15.2024.03.03_attempted_50words.mat` (10 ms bins, raw) against card_2024 session `t15.2024.03.03`, train split (20 ms bins, z-scored). kunz was rebinned to 20 ms.

- All 200 kunz trials pair with a distinct card trial of the same text. The summed crossings of each pair correlate at 0.71 or more (median 0.81).
- With one fixed 20 ms bin phase for the whole kunz file, both features give the identity assignment on all 256 columns, with median diagonal correlation 0.63 (crossings) and 0.69 (power). Crossings are also the identity as best match. For power, card columns 14, 129 and 234 correlate slightly more with columns 6, 138 and 237 of the same array. Two random halves of the trials give the same map.
- Choosing the 10 ms bin phase per trial, and standardizing each trial since card is z-scored, raises the median diagonal correlation to 1.00 for both features (minimum 0.99). All 256 columns are then the identity as best match and as assignment.
- Controls: pairing each kunz trial with the next card trial drops the median diagonal correlation to 0.01 (crossings) and 0.06 (power), and the identity assignment to 14% and 27% of columns. A known random permutation of the kunz columns is recovered in full.

### card_2024 and wairagkar_2025, 25 shared days

wairagkar_2025 (10 ms bins, log-transformed, normalized over a rolling 10 s window, causally smoothed over 1.5 s) against card_2024 train and validation splits on the same day. card was repeated to 10 ms. The main run leaves card unsmoothed, and a second run smooths card with a causal 0.5 s ramp.

- 4,849 pairs over the 25 days (5,682 card train and validation trials, 5,798 wairagkar trials). 5 of them are different recordings of the same sentence (summed-crossing correlation -0.12 to 0.49), too few to change the results below.
- Threshold crossings: the identity on all 256 columns, as best match and as assignment, pooled over all days, on each day alone, and on alternating sessions. Median diagonal correlation 0.55, smallest margin over the best other column 0.31.
- Spike-band power: the identity assignment on all 256 columns under the same splits. As best match, all 256 columns are the identity pooled and on alternating sessions. On 14 of the 25 days, 1-3 columns correlate slightly more (by at most 0.015) with another column. Median diagonal correlation 0.58, smallest pooled margin 0.12.
- The 0.5 s smoothing gives the same identity for both features (median diagonal 0.66 and 0.74). On five days, subtracting each trial's mean or taking first differences keeps the identity for both features.
- Controls: pairing each wairagkar trial with the next card trial drops the median diagonal correlation to 0.007 (crossings) and 0.02 (power). The assignment still keeps 86% and 95% of columns in place, so channel structure shared by all trials of a day survives wrong pairings. The evidence for the map is the diagonal correlation and its margin, not the assignment alone. A known random permutation is recovered in full.

## Within each release

Threshold crossings and spike-band power share one column order in each release. On the same samples, the assignment between a release's crossings and its power is the identity for 92-100% of columns across ten card sessions from 2023-08-11 to 2025-01-12, 97% in kunz (2024-03-03) and 98% in wairagkar (day 195). As best match the figures are 89-99%, 88% and 94%. The exceptions are columns whose power correlates with other columns. card and kunz have the same 8 assignment exceptions on 2024-03-03, as expected from the same recording.

## Structure-based checks

These compare each release's own channel-by-channel correlation matrix, so they also apply to trials that were not recorded in both releases. They can only give `candidate` results.

- Array order. Keeping each array's internal order, the current block order ranks first among the 24 possible orders of the four arrays in every comparison: card and kunz on the same trials and on different trials of 2024-03-03, card and kunz on 2024-06-14 (kunz `interleavedVerbalBehaviors`), and wairagkar day 195 (2024-01-28) against kunz 2024-03-03, for both features.
- Electrode level. Graph matching (`scipy.optimize.quadratic_assignment`) of the correlation matrices recovers 78-87% of columns from a known permutation of one card session split in halves, and 87% (crossings) and 70% (power) between card and kunz on the same 2024-03-03 trials. Between different tasks it fails: 4-11% for card against kunz imagined-speech trials of the same day, 3-4% for card and kunz on 2024-06-14, and 1-4% for wairagkar against kunz across days. These comparisons therefore say nothing about electrode identity.

## Across sessions

Graph matching of consecutive sessions of the same task recovers a median of 9% of columns in card_2024 (range 1-68%) and 26% in wairagkar_2025 (0-66%), against 0.4% for chance and about 80% within one session. The range includes card sessions 2023-10-20 and 2023-10-22 at 2%, two days that each match wairagkar_2025 channel for channel. Changes in neural structure, signal quality or task between sessions can all lower the recovery, so this check cannot confirm or refute a stable channel order. Whether a column is the same electrode on two different days is a property of the recording rig shared by all three releases, and it applies equally to pooling days within one dataset.

## singer_2025

singer_2025 shares one day with the other releases: post-implant day 39 (2023-08-25), also recorded in card_2024 and wairagkar_2025. No trials are shared. The singer blocks of that day are cursor tasks, the others are speech, and the singer files carry no recording clock to align blocks with. Its other days, 202 (2024-02-04) and 468 (2024-10-27), are in no other release. All checks here are therefore structure-based, and at the electrode level they give candidate results only.

Every singer block carries `array_label_by_electrode`, identical in all 51 blocks: `v6v` for columns 0-63, `4` for 64-127, `55b` for 128-191 and `d6v` for 192-255. The Dryad README describes the field, and the authors' code (`Neuroprosthetics-Lab/BCI-cursor-control-from-speech-motor-cortex`, commit `6171cfa`) uses it to label electrodes. The paper refers to Card et al. supplementary appendix S1.02 for the arrays. No source states an electrode-level map.

The checks are those of [Structure-based checks](#structure-based-checks), on all blocks of each day at 20 ms bins, with 300 random within-array orders as the null for the correlation matrices and 1,000 for the mean levels. The within-array test uses the centred score of [t5.md](t5.md) and [t17.md](t17.md), with each array-pair submatrix's mean removed, against 500 random within-array orders.

- Calibration on alternate blocks of each singer day. The stored order ranks first of the 24 array orders and is far above every within-array order. Graph matching returns 58-96% of columns to their own position without smoothing and 91-98% with 50 ms smoothing.
- Array order. Without smoothing, the stored order ranks first of 24 for both features against card and wairagkar on day 39, against wairagkar day 195, card 2024-02-25 and kunz 2024-03-03 for day 202, and against kunz 2024-12-15 and card 2025-01-10 for day 468. With 50 ms smoothing it ranks first in all of them except day 468 against card 2025-01-10 for spike-band power, where it ranks second.
- Within-array order. With the centred score and without smoothing, the stored order beats all 500 within-array orders against each of these releases, for both features: 0.25-0.53 against at most 0.10, 14 to 36 standard deviations above the mean of the random orders. The same recordings in card and wairagkar on day 39 give 0.91 (crossings) and 0.83 (power), and the halves of one singer day 0.84-0.99. Between singer days the stored order also beats all 500 (0.06-0.39 against at most 0.05).
- Graph matching. Across releases it recovers 0-4% of columns, including the same-day comparisons with card and wairagkar. The controls recover 100% (crossings) and 78% (power) for card and wairagkar on the same day 39 recordings, 59% and 45% for card 2023-08-25 against 2023-08-27, and 10% and 21% for kunz 2024-12-15 against 2025-01-05. As in the other releases, graph matching between different tasks says nothing about electrode identity.
- Mean level per column. singer and kunz both release unnormalized features, so their mean levels can be compared column by column. Singer day 468 against kunz 2024-12-15 (49 days apart) correlates 0.61 for crossings and 0.80 for power, and against kunz 2025-01-05 0.55 and 0.73. Singer day 202 against kunz 2024-03-03 gives 0.46 and 0.67. The within-array orders reach at most 0.27 and 0.34. For comparison, kunz against itself gives 0.74 and 0.86 at 21 days apart and 0.26 and 0.55 at 287 days. Within singer, days 202 and 468 give 0.28 and 0.44, while days 39 and 202 give 0.08 and 0.10, below the within-array orders.
- Crossings and power share one column order. The assignment between them is the identity for 99%, 95% and 91% of columns on days 39, 202 and 468.

The array order of singer_2025 is verified: the source labels state it and the data agree. The electrode order within each array is a candidate. The stored order matches the other releases better than any within-array reordering, and its mean levels agree with kunz, but no recording is shared that could confirm individual electrodes.

## Notes for use

- Pool T15 columns by index across the three datasets. On shared days this is verified, elsewhere it rests on the stable layout the sources state.
- Processing differs: card is 20 ms, z-scored over the preceding 20 trials and clipped at 10 SD. wairagkar is 10 ms, log-transformed, normalized over a rolling 10 s window and smoothed over 1.5 s. kunz is 10 ms and raw. Rescaling each dataset does not undo these differences. The source decoder inputs order the features differently (card crossings then power, wairagkar power then crossings).
- `card_2024/utils.ARRAY_ORDER` labels the arrays `"ventral 6v", "area 4", "55b", "dorsal 6v"`, while the dataset metadata uses `ventral_6v`, `4`, `55b`, `dorsal_6v`.
- singer_2025 is 10 ms and raw, like kunz.
- The wairagkar_2025 control experiment declares the same layout and is not covered here.
